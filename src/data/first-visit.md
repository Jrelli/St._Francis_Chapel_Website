## The Holy Sacrifice of the Mass

The Holy Sacrifice of the Mass is, first and foremost, the worship of Almighty God. It is not centered upon man, his preferences, or his desire to be entertained, but upon the honor, glory, and adoration due to the Most Holy Trinity.

In an age when so much of life is directed toward ourselves, the Traditional Latin Mass stands as a profound reminder that we were created to know, love, and serve God.

The Mass is not merely a gathering of the faithful, nor is it simply a remembrance of an event long past. It is the same Sacrifice of Our Lord Jesus Christ upon Calvary, made present in an unbloody manner upon the altar. Through the sacred ministry of the priest, acting <em lang="la">in persona Christi</em>, the one perfect Sacrifice of Christ is offered to the Eternal Father.

Every prayer, gesture, ceremony, and moment of silence is ordered toward this sacred reality. The beauty of the vestments, the solemnity of the Latin prayers, the fragrance of incense, the sound of Gregorian chant, and the profound silence surrounding the consecration all serve one purpose: to direct the soul away from worldly distractions and toward the worship of God.

The Traditional Latin Mass is a venerable inheritance of Holy Mother Church, handed down through generations and lovingly preserved. Its sacred rites express the Catholic faith with remarkable clarity, teaching us through word and gesture the mysteries we profess.

For those assisting at the Traditional Latin Mass for the first time, much may seem unfamiliar. This is to be expected. The sacred liturgy is not something to be mastered in a single visit, nor is it intended to conform itself to our personal expectations.

Rather, we must learn to conform ourselves to the spirit of the liturgy: humility before God, reverence in His presence, and a prayerful disposition of heart.

We come to Holy Mass not to be entertained, but to adore Almighty God, to unite ourselves to the Sacrifice of His Divine Son, and to receive the graces He so generously bestows.

## What to Expect at the Traditional Latin Mass

### 1. The Sacred Language and Prayerful Silence

The Traditional Latin Mass is celebrated almost entirely in Latin, the sacred liturgical language of the Roman Church.

Latin serves as a visible expression of the unity and continuity of the Catholic faith across nations and centuries. Its use also helps preserve the sacred character of the liturgy, setting the worship of God apart from the ordinary activities of daily life.

You are not expected to understand every Latin prayer immediately.

Missals may be used to follow the priest as he offers the Holy Sacrifice. These ordinarily contain the prayers of the Mass in both Latin and English. Certain prayers, known as the Propers, change according to the feast or liturgical day and may not be included in simpler missals.

There is no need to be troubled by this. The priest offers these prayers on behalf of the faithful, and it is not necessary for everyone to follow every word or action in order to assist fruitfully at Holy Mass.

One of the most striking characteristics of the Traditional Latin Mass is its profound silence.

Many of the prayers, particularly those of the Sacred Canon, are recited quietly by the priest. This silence is deliberate. It expresses the awe, humility, and adoration with which we should approach the Sacred Mysteries.

Rather than filling every moment with spoken words, the liturgy allows the soul to contemplate the mystery unfolding upon the altar.

The faithful are encouraged to unite themselves interiorly to the prayers of the priest, meditate upon the Passion of Our Lord, and offer their own intentions in union with the Holy Sacrifice.

Silence in the presence of God is not emptiness. It is an act of reverence and an invitation to prayer.

### 2. Posture and Participation in the Sacred Liturgy

Those attending the Traditional Latin Mass for the first time may be unfamiliar with the customary times for standing, sitting, and kneeling.

There is no need for concern. Simply observe the congregation and follow their example.

It may be helpful to sit toward the rear of the chapel, where the actions of others may be more easily observed.

Participation in the Traditional Latin Mass is principally interior.

The faithful are not required to recite every prayer aloud, follow every movement of the priest, or maintain the same outward activity throughout the liturgy.

Our participation consists above all in uniting our hearts and minds to the Sacrifice being offered, adoring Our Lord truly present upon the altar, and offering ourselves to God in union with His Divine Son.

The priest acts in his sacred office as the minister of Christ, offering the Holy Sacrifice on behalf of the Church. The faithful assist by joining themselves prayerfully to this offering.

At certain moments, the most fitting participation is simply to kneel in silence and adore.

The value of our assistance at Holy Mass is not measured by the number of words we speak or the number of actions we perform, but by our faith, devotion, and interior union with the Sacrifice of Christ.

### 3. The Priest and the Sacred Altar

In the Traditional Latin Mass, the priest ordinarily faces the altar, in the same direction as the congregation. This traditional orientation is known as <em lang="la">ad orientem</em>, or "toward the East."

It is sometimes mistakenly understood as the priest turning his back upon the faithful. In reality, priest and people are united in a common direction of worship, turned toward Almighty God.

The priest does not stand at the altar as a performer addressing an audience. He stands as the sacred minister of Christ, offering the Holy Sacrifice to the Eternal Father.

Throughout the Mass, you will notice numerous bows, genuflections, signs of the Cross, and other prescribed gestures. These are not unnecessary embellishments. They are outward expressions of the Church's faith in the Real Presence of Our Lord and in the holiness of the mysteries being celebrated.

During the Sacred Canon, the priest prays in a low voice, emphasizing the solemnity of the action taking place.

At the consecration, through the words of Christ spoken by the priest, the bread and wine become the true Body and Blood of Our Lord Jesus Christ, while retaining their appearances.

The elevation of the Sacred Host and the Precious Blood invites the faithful to adore their Eucharistic Lord, truly present upon the altar.

At the center of the Mass stands not the congregation, nor even the priest himself, but Jesus Christ, our Eternal High Priest and Divine Victim.

### 4. Gregorian Chant and Sacred Music

Sacred music has always held an honored place in the worship of Holy Mother Church.

At the Sung Mass, Gregorian chant and traditional sacred polyphony give voice to the Church's prayers, raising the mind and heart toward heavenly things.

Gregorian chant is particularly suited to the Roman liturgy because of its sacred character, restraint, and close union with the liturgical texts.

Unlike music intended primarily for entertainment or emotional stimulation, sacred music exists to glorify God and assist the faithful in prayer.

At a Low Mass, there may be no music at all. The quiet prayers of the priest, the responses of the servers, and the periods of silence create an atmosphere of recollection and devotion.

Whether accompanied by sacred chant or offered in near silence, the Holy Sacrifice remains the same.

### 5. The Reception of Holy Communion

The Most Holy Eucharist is the true Body, Blood, Soul, and Divinity of Our Lord Jesus Christ.

For this reason, the reception of Holy Communion must always be approached with the greatest reverence, humility, and proper spiritual preparation.

At Saint Francis Mission Chapel, Holy Communion is received kneeling at the altar rail and directly upon the tongue, unless a physical impediment prevents kneeling.

The Sacred Host is placed upon the tongue by the priest. Communicants do not extend their hands to receive and do not respond "Amen," as the priest recites the traditional formula of administration.

Those approaching Holy Communion must be baptized Catholics, properly disposed, in a state of sanctifying grace, and observing the prescribed Eucharistic fast.

Anyone conscious of having committed a mortal sin must first receive sacramental absolution in Confession before approaching Holy Communion.

Those who are not Catholic, who are not properly disposed, or who do not intend to receive Holy Communion should remain in their pews.

There is no expectation that every person present must receive Communion at every Mass. Indeed, the obligation of the faithful is to assist at Holy Mass on Sundays and holy days of obligation, while the reception of Holy Communion is governed by its own requirements.

If you remain in your pew, you may use this sacred time to adore Our Lord and make an act of spiritual communion, expressing your desire to be united with Him.

The decision to refrain from receiving when one is not properly prepared is itself an act of reverence toward the Blessed Sacrament.

### 6. Modesty and Proper Attire in the House of God

The church is the House of God, consecrated to His worship and sanctified by the Real Presence of Our Lord in the Blessed Sacrament.

Our outward appearance should reflect the respect, modesty, and reverence appropriate to so sacred a place.

The manner in which we dress for Holy Mass is not simply a matter of personal taste. It is an outward expression of the dignity of divine worship and of our recognition that we stand in the presence of Almighty God.

At Saint Francis Mission Chapel, traditional standards of modest and dignified attire are expected.

#### For Men

Men should wear dress trousers and dress shirts. Ties, suit coats, and other formal attire are common and appropriate.

Jeans, shorts, T-shirts, and other overly casual clothing are not appropriate for assisting at Holy Mass.

Men are also expected to remove hats and other head coverings upon entering the chapel.

#### For Women

Women should wear modest dresses or skirts reaching at least to the knee, together with tops that adequately cover the shoulders and neckline.

In keeping with the traditional custom of the Church, women ordinarily wear a chapel veil or another modest head covering while in church.

These customs are not intended to draw attention to outward appearances, but rather to preserve the dignity and reverence befitting the House of God.

Modesty directs attention away from ourselves and toward the sacred purpose for which we have gathered: the worship of Almighty God.

### 7. Reverence and Conduct Within the Chapel

From the moment we enter the chapel, we should be mindful that we are entering a sacred place set apart for the worship of God.

Before entering the pew, it is customary to genuflect toward the tabernacle in acknowledgment of the Real Presence of Our Lord in the Blessed Sacrament. Those physically unable to genuflect may make a reverent bow.

Silence should be observed within the chapel, particularly before and after Holy Mass, so that the faithful may prepare themselves in prayer and offer thanksgiving afterward.

Conversations and social greetings should be reserved for outside the chapel.

Mobile phones and other electronic devices should be silenced and put away.

Parents are encouraged to teach their children the importance of reverence in church, according to their age and ability. The presence of children is a blessing, and families are an important part of parish life.

These practices help preserve the atmosphere of prayer and recollection that properly belongs to the House of God.

## Growing in Understanding and Devotion

For those who are accustomed to more contemporary forms of worship, the Traditional Latin Mass may require a period of adjustment.

Its sacred language, solemn ceremonies, silence, and deliberate movements are not designed around the habits and expectations of the modern world.

They are the inheritance of a Church that has sought, throughout the centuries, to offer Almighty God worship marked by beauty, dignity, sacrifice, and reverence.

The richness of the Traditional Latin Mass is not always immediately apparent. Its beauty often unfolds gradually as we become familiar with its prayers, understand its sacred symbolism, and grow in the interior dispositions necessary for fruitful participation.

It is therefore wise not to judge the liturgy by the impressions of a single visit.

Attend regularly. Learn the prayers. Study the meaning of the ceremonies. Above all, cultivate a spirit of humility and prayer.

In time, the profound silence that may initially seem unfamiliar can become a treasured opportunity for contemplation. The ancient Latin prayers become familiar expressions of the Church's faith. The sacred gestures reveal more clearly the mysteries taking place upon the altar.

Yet even as our understanding deepens, we must never forget that the Mass is infinitely greater than our ability to comprehend it.

The mysteries of God are not diminished by our inability to grasp them fully.

We come before Him in faith, acknowledging His infinite majesty and our dependence upon His grace.

## Welcome to Saint Francis Mission Chapel

We are grateful for your interest in the Traditional Latin Mass and welcome you to assist at the Holy Sacrifice with us.

Whether you are attending for the first time or returning to the ancient liturgy of the Roman Church after many years, we encourage you to approach the Mass with humility, patience, and a sincere desire to worship God.

Do not be discouraged if you do not understand every prayer or ceremony. Do not concern yourself with perfectly following every action of the priest.

The essential thing is to recognize where you are and what is taking place.

You are in the House of God, assisting at the Holy Sacrifice in which Our Lord Jesus Christ offers Himself to the Eternal Father for the salvation of souls.

We do not come to Holy Mass to be entertained, to be affirmed in our preferences, or to place ourselves at the center of worship.

We come to adore Almighty God, to offer Him the worship that is His due, and to unite ourselves to the perfect Sacrifice of His Divine Son.

May Our Blessed Lady, Saint Joseph, and Saint Francis of Assisi intercede for us, that we may always assist at the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass with the reverence, humility, and devotion befitting so great a mystery.
